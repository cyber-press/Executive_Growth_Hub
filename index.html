Startup Guide

Start with the right foundation.

A strong business starts before the logo, website, or first sale. Use this section to move from a rough idea to a clear, testable, and organized business foundation.

Download Startup Checklist
Before you launch: validate demand, define your offer, confirm basic registration needs, separate money tracking, and write a 90-day action plan.

1Clarify the business idea

Write the idea in one sentence so you can explain it quickly to customers, partners, and supporters.

  • Define the problem: What pain, delay, cost, or frustration are you solving?
  • Define the result: What will the customer gain after using your product or service?
  • Check demand: Ask at least 10 potential customers what they currently use and what they would pay for.

2Understand your audience

The right audience makes marketing easier because you know who you are speaking to and what they care about.

  • Profile the customer: Location, income level, habits, goals, pain points, and decision triggers.
  • Find where they gather: WhatsApp groups, Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, markets, churches, events, or professional networks.
  • Study objections: List why they may delay buying and prepare trust-building answers.

3Shape a clear offer

Your offer should make it obvious what the customer receives, how it is delivered, and why it is worth the price.

  • Name the offer: Give each service, package, or product a clear name.
  • State what is included: Deliverables, timeline, support, quantity, and expected outcome.
  • Set pricing logic: Consider cost, time, profit margin, competitor pricing, and perceived value.

4Build basic credibility

People need proof before they trust a new business. Start with simple assets that make you look organized and reachable.

  • Create contact channels: Business email, WhatsApp Business, phone number, and social pages.
  • Prepare brand basics: Logo, colors, short bio, service description, and customer promise.
  • Add proof: Photos, samples, testimonials, certificates, before-and-after results, or founder story.

5Set up money tracking

Financial discipline protects your business from confusion and helps you know whether the business is actually profitable.

  • Separate money: Use a dedicated bank account or clearly separated tracking sheet.
  • Track every movement: Income, expenses, receipts, supplier costs, delivery costs, and owner withdrawals.
  • Know your break-even point: Calculate the sales needed to cover monthly expenses.

6Create a 90-day action plan

Do not try to build everything at once. Use a 90-day plan to focus on the most important launch actions.

  • Month 1: Validate idea, define audience, build offer, estimate costs.
  • Month 2: Set up brand, registration plan, online presence, and sample content.
  • Month 3: Launch, collect feedback, improve offer, track sales, and review results.

Financial & Legal Readiness

Prepare your business the right way.

Practical readiness guidance for Nigeria-focused founders and members operating in the United States. Confirm details with official portals before filing.

Important: This is general business information, not legal, tax, or financial advice. Government processes and fees can change, so use the official links provided and verify current requirements before submitting applications or payments.
🇳🇬 Nigeria Resource

CACBusiness registration in Nigeria

Most formal businesses in Nigeria start by registering with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC). Registration helps your business become legally recognized, verifiable, and better positioned for bank accounts, contracts, grants, and partnerships.

  • Choose the right structure: Business Name for small sole proprietorships; Limited Liability Company (LLC) for structured enterprises; Incorporated Trustees for nonprofits/foundations.
  • Search and reserve a name: Check if the name is available and reserve it through the CAC portal.
  • Complete the online form: Fill the required pre-registration form and provide owner/director/trustee information as applicable.
  • Upload documents: Add identity documents, signatures, addresses, passport photos, or other required documents based on the structure.
  • Pay filing fees: Submit payment through the official CAC portal and track the application.
  • Download certificate: After approval, save the electronic certificate and registration extract for bank, tax, and compliance use.
CAC Business Name Steps CAC Registration Portal CAC Company Search
🇺🇸 U.S. Resource

USAU.S. business setup basics

Members operating in the United States should confirm requirements for their state, city, county, tax status, and business structure before accepting customers publicly.

  • Choose a structure: Decide whether you are operating as a sole proprietorship, LLC, S-Corp, C-Corp, partnership, or 501(c)(3) nonprofit.
  • Register with your state: Use your Secretary of State or state business portal for formation, name search, and filing requirements.
  • Get an EIN: Apply online through the IRS if you need a federal Employer Identification Number for banking, hiring, or corporate tax filings.
  • Check licenses and permits: Review local city, county, and industry permits before selling regulated products or services.
  • Set up tax and records: Track income, expenses, receipts, sales tax obligations, payroll needs, and annual reports.
SBA Start a Business IRS EIN Application IRS Small Business Tax Center

TINTax readiness and TIN

After registration, your business should be ready to handle tax records properly. A Tax Identification Number and accurate bookkeeping make it easier to file taxes, open accounts, apply for opportunities, and avoid confusion later.

  • Confirm your TIN status: CAC-registered entities may be linked to tax records, but you should verify through the official tax portal.
  • Create or access TaxPro Max: Use FIRS TaxPro Max to register, manage filings, validate TCC, and access tax services.
  • Keep clean records: Separate income, expenses, receipts, invoices, payroll, and supplier payments.
  • Understand tax types: Depending on your business, taxes may include company income tax, VAT, withholding tax, PAYE, or state-level obligations.
FIRS TaxPro Max TaxPro Registration Source

SMESMEDAN & MSME Support

SMEDAN can be useful for small and medium businesses looking for training, funding opportunities, government/private sector support, and enterprise development resources.

  • Register with SMEDAN: Create a business profile and keep your information current.
  • Watch for programs: Look out for training, grants, loans, insurance support, and capacity-building opportunities.
  • Use it as credibility: Keep your SMEDAN registration and business information organized for opportunities.
  • Combine with CAC: SMEDAN support is helpful, but it does not replace proper CAC registration where required.
SMEDAN Register Now SMEDAN Portal

LICLicenses and permits

Some businesses need more than CAC registration. Permits depend on your industry, products, state, location, and whether you manufacture, import, sell regulated goods, or operate from a physical premises.

  • Food, drugs, cosmetics, medical devices: Check NAFDAC product registration before manufacturing, importing, advertising, selling, or distributing.
  • Manufacturing and product quality: Review SON requirements, product certification, and standards that apply to locally made or imported goods.
  • Business premises: Many states require business premises registration or local government/state permits for physical locations.
NAFDAC Portal SON Certification NIPC Portal

₦Separate finances and bookkeeping

Good financial habits help business owners know what is coming in, what is going out, and what profit remains.

  • Open or dedicate an account: Keep business money away from personal spending.
  • Track cash daily: Record sales, expenses, transfers, refunds, and unpaid invoices.
  • Save receipts: Store digital copies in Google Drive, Dropbox, WhatsApp folder, or bookkeeping software.
  • Review monthly: Compare income, expenses, profit, debt, inventory, and savings.
